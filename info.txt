COMMONPLACE is a folder on my desktop.

Anything I drop into it, a photo, a GIF, a line I overheard in a dream, shows up here about a minute later. There is no app, no upload screen.

It borrows from the commonplace book: the notebooks writers, scholars and tinkerers kept for centuries, copying in passages, recipes, quotes and observations worth coming back to. A commonplace book isn't organized by a system. It's organized by the act of collecting, by whatever caught your eye that week. This works the same way.

How it works: my Mac watches the folder. When a file lands, a small script makes a web-sized copy, adds it to a list, and pushes it to GitHub, which hosts the page. Text lives in a single plain notebook file that stays in sync. Fix a typo on my laptop and the site follows. The file system is the CMS. Git is the database. The folder is the interface.
